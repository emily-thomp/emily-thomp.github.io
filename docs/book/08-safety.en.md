# Safety and worker protection

The following is a translation of the original 1958 text, including its period-specific protective practices.

1. During ammonia oxidation, ammonia concentration must not exceed 12%, because of explosion danger. Excessive concentration also lowers conversion.
2. When igniting with a hydrogen flame, the text calls for purging with hydrogen first to avoid an explosion of a hydrogen–air mixture during ignition. During catalyst ignition or heating, operators are to wear protective goggles and an ammonia-protective mask. If no such mask is available, the original proposes covering the mouth and nose with a wet towel to prevent sudden ammonia release from overcoming the operator. It recommends a larger negative pressure in the oxidizer during ignition to prevent ammonia escape, and careful, limited opening of the ammonia valve.
3. Catalyst temperature must not exceed 820°C, because excessive temperature damages the catalyst.
4. Wear protective goggles when operating the absorption system to prevent acid splashes entering the eyes.
5. Wear rubber gloves when filling nitric acid containers to avoid burns. For an accidental splash, the original specifies immediate washing with clean water, or with dilute alkaline water followed by clean water.
6. When filling liquid ammonia, prevent leakage at connecting pipes so that liquid ammonia does not rush out of the cylinder. After connecting a cylinder to the pressure-buffer vessel, open the cylinder valve slightly first and check for leakage before opening further.
7. Do not expose liquid-ammonia cylinders to direct sunlight: excessive temperature raises cylinder pressure and creates explosion danger.
