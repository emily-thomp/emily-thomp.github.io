# Overview and front matter

## Title and publication

**Nitric Acid Production by Ammonia Oxidation Using Locally Made Equipment**

Compiled by **Wu Yayi**. Published by **Keji Weisheng Publishing House**, Shanghai, December 1958.

### Summary

This book describes the production of nitric acid by catalytic oxidation of ammonia using locally made equipment. It covers the production flowsheet, drawings and materials of individual units, their functions, operating and control methods, analytical methods, and catalyst manufacture. It was intended as a reference for factories making their own nitric acid and for operators of these small plants.

### Publication details

The publisher's address was 2004 Nanjing West Road, Shanghai. The original lists a Shanghai book-publishing business licence, printing by the Shanghai Printing Works of Zhonghua Book Company, and distribution by the Shanghai Distribution Office of Xinhua Bookstore.

## Preface

Nitric acid can be produced by distilling concentrated sulfuric acid with saltpetre, here sodium nitrate. Although the equipment and operation are simple, supplies of concentrated sulfuric acid and sodium nitrate were insufficient to meet the requirements of other industries, making widespread adoption difficult.

This book therefore introduces ammonia oxidation with locally made equipment. Iron–bismuth oxides replace platinum–rhodium gauze, which was then difficult to obtain domestically, and ceramic equipment replaces stainless-steel absorption towers. Most equipment materials could be prepared locally, while instruments and the remaining items were available on the market. Plants could consequently be built relatively easily and with little capital investment. The process, individual units, operating controls and catalyst preparation have been assembled here to help factories and mines produce their own nitric acid.

Most material comes from actual small-scale nitric acid production at the Shanghai Chemical Industry Research Institute, Shanghai Tiangong Acid and Alkali Works, and other plants. References also included *Nitric Acid* by Shinichi Uchida and others, published by Sangyo Tosho in Japan in 1955, and *Nitric Acid Engineering* by Zhou Jiayong, published by the Commercial Press in 1954. The author acknowledges limited production experience and a short compilation period, and invites criticism and correction of errors and omissions.

Wu Yayi, 28 October 1958.

## Overview

Nitric acid is an important chemical product, second only to sulfuric acid in the scale of production among acids. Nitric acid and nitrates have major significance in the national economy, particularly agriculture and national defence. Ammonium nitrate, produced by neutralizing nitric acid with ammonia, is a useful fertilizer containing 35% nitrogen. Its nitrogen is quickly absorbed by plants and benefits cotton, flax, tobacco and vegetables. A large share of ammonium nitrate is also used for smokeless propellants or mixed explosives for water-conservancy construction and mining. These applications require considerable quantities of nitric acid.

Nitric acid is used to manufacture photographic and pharmaceutical chemicals, and organic dyes. It is also used in sulfuric acid production to assist oxidation of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide, which is then absorbed in water.

As the Great Leap Forward in industry and agriculture gathered pace, demand for nitric acid increased. Many large nitric acid plants had recently been expanded or built, yet output still lagged behind rapidly growing production requirements.

The need for stainless steel and platinum-alloy gauze restricted wider development of the industry. It was commonly thought that nitric acid could not be produced with locally made equipment on a widespread basis. Encouraged by the Party Central Committee's call for small local plants and service to the “steel commander,” however, the authors describe overcoming these assumptions and achieving local production within a short period. The following sections introduce the equipment, process, operation and analysis for readers engaged in this work.
