# Operating methods

## Ammonia oxidizer

In the oxidation section, regularly check ammonia and air flows to the reactor and maintain an ammonia concentration of about 9.5–10.5%. With a single platinum gauze combined with a non-platinum metal-oxide catalyst, such as iron–bismuth or iron–chromium, the stated range is about 10–10.5%. With a non-platinum catalyst alone, maintain about 10%. Excessive concentration lowers oxidation efficiency.

The control method is to establish the air flow first and then adjust ammonia admission. For 500 kg of liquid ammonia per day, the hourly ammonia gas flow is:

$$
\frac{500\times22.4}{17\times24}=27.5\ \mathrm{m^3/h}
$$

At 10% ammonia concentration, the corresponding air flow is:

$$
\frac{27.5-(27.5\times0.1)}{0.1}=247.5\ \mathrm{m^3/h}
$$

Flowmeters may have errors, so analyse combined nitrogen in the ammonia–air mixture to check whether concentration is being controlled correctly. The combined-nitrogen result obtained during efficiency analysis can also be used to look up ammonia concentration in the following table.

Reaction temperature is another important control point and is indicated by the installed thermocouple. The text gives about 750°C for a combined platinum/non-platinum bed and about 700°C for a non-platinum bed alone, depending on the catalyst's properties.

Temperature is controlled through air preheat. At normal ammonia concentration, reduce preheat if reaction temperature is too high and increase it if too low. Adjust the air passing through the heat exchanger: opening the exchanger air valve wider increases the amount of heated air and raises preheat temperature.

Only oxidation-efficiency analysis establishes how well the catalyst is reacting. During normal operation, analyse every four hours; during production changes, analyse more frequently. The reported efficiency is about 95–96% with a combined bed and about 70–85% with a non-platinum bed alone.

## Absorption system

At startup, the acid in the towers is weak, so additional water is initially unnecessary. After several hours, when the first tower reaches the required concentration, generally 45%, introduce water into the last tower. Adjust this water flow according to acid concentration from the first tower. Increase it slightly if the acid reaches or rises above 45%; reduce it if concentration falls. Check the first-tower concentration regularly with a hydrometer.

If a tail-gas recovery tower is installed, use an alkaline solution to absorb nitrogen oxides. This improves recovery and also yields some sodium nitrite. The book describes circulating 30% alkaline solution and replacing it when alkalinity falls to about 3%.

Lower temperatures favour absorption. Supply sufficient cooling water to all coolers and attend to its adjustment.
