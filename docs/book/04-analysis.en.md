# Process-control analysis

## Ammonia oxidation efficiency

Ammonia oxidation efficiency is the fraction of ammonia converted to nitric oxide on passage over the catalyst. Some ammonia instead forms nitrogen. The book gives the reactions as:

Formation of nitric oxide:

$$
4\mathrm{NH_3}+5\mathrm{O_2}=4\mathrm{NO}+6\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

Formation of nitrogen:

$$
4\mathrm{NH_3}+3\mathrm{O_2}=2\mathrm{N_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

Measure the percentage of chemically combined nitrogen in the ammonia–air mixture upstream of the catalyst and in the nitrogen-oxide mixture downstream. The ratio of the two values gives the oxidation efficiency.

The analysis described uses two 500 mL evacuated glass bulbs. Rinse them three times with distilled water, dry the glass stopcocks, apply a thin coating of petroleum jelly, and evacuate with a vacuum pump for about 4–5 minutes. Check the stopcocks carefully for leakage, wipe the bulb surfaces with clean fine cloth, and weigh accurately.

At the sampling openings of the oxidizer, take an upstream and a downstream sample into the weighed bulbs. Weigh them again; the difference is the sample mass. Introduce distilled water and shake vigorously. After the gases have been absorbed, transfer the contents to separate 500 mL conical flasks, taking care not to allow residual gas to escape. Repeat three times until all gas has been absorbed; the combined washings amount to about 200 mL. Add 2–4 drops of methyl red to each and titrate. The ammonia–air sample is titrated with 0.1 N standard sulfuric acid and the nitrogen-oxide sample with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide.

Combined nitrogen in the ammonia–air sample, %:

$$
\frac{N_{\mathrm{H_2SO_4}}\times V_{\mathrm{H_2SO_4}}\times\frac{14}{1000}}{W_1}\times100\qquad(1)
$$

Combined nitrogen in the nitrogen-oxide sample, %:

$$
\frac{N_{\mathrm{NaOH}}\times V_{\mathrm{NaOH}}\times\frac{14}{1000}}{W_2}\times100\qquad(2)
$$

$$
\text{Oxidation efficiency}\%=\frac{\text{Combined nitrogen in nitrogen-oxide mixture}}{\text{Combined nitrogen in ammonia–air mixture}}\times100
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $N_{\mathrm{H_2SO_4}}$ | Normality of the sulfuric acid titrant |
| $V_{\mathrm{H_2SO_4}}$ | Sulfuric acid used, mL |
| $N_{\mathrm{NaOH}}$ | Normality of the sodium hydroxide titrant |
| $V_{\mathrm{NaOH}}$ | Sodium hydroxide used, mL |
| $W_1$ | Mass of the ammonia–air sample, g |
| $W_2$ | Mass of the nitrogen-oxide sample, g |

## Nitric acid concentration

The concentration of acid from the absorption towers is to be measured regularly. The locally equipped process generally produces acid at 45%; with good cooling, the text reports up to 49%. Take a sample from the first absorption tower in a 200 mL measuring cylinder, measure its specific gravity with a hydrometer, and obtain its concentration from the table.
